## Why Reporting Changes Matters

When you receive SNAP benefits, you are required to report certain changes in your household to your local SNAP office. According to USDA Food and Nutrition Service (FNS), failing to report changes on time can lead to overpayments you may have to pay back, underpayments that shortchange your household, or potential disqualification from the program.

## What Changes Must Be Reported

Most states require you to report changes that affect your eligibility or benefit amount. Common reportable changes include:

- **Income changes** — new job, job loss, raise, reduced hours, or new sources of income (child support, Social Security, etc.)
- **Household changes** — someone moves in or out, a birth, a death, or a marriage/separation
- **Address changes** — moving to a new home, even within the same county
- **Resource changes** — significant changes to bank accounts or other countable assets (rules vary by state)

Some states use "simplified reporting," which means you only need to report when your gross income exceeds a certain threshold or at periodic intervals. Your state SNAP office will tell you which reporting rules apply to your case.

## When and How to Report

In most states you must report changes within 10 days of the date the change happens, according to FNS guidance. You can typically report changes by calling your caseworker, visiting your local office, mailing a change report form, or using your state's online benefits portal. Keep copies or screenshots of anything you submit for your own records.

*For official eligibility information and state-specific reporting rules, contact your state SNAP office or visit [benefits.gov](https://benefits.gov).*
