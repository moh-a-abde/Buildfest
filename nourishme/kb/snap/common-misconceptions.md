## Myth: SNAP Can Only Buy Junk Food

**Fact:** SNAP benefits can be used to buy almost any food item for home consumption, including fruits, vegetables, meat, dairy, bread, cereals, snacks, and non-alcoholic beverages. According to USDA FNS, there are no restrictions on buying healthy versus unhealthy food — the choice is yours. Many participants use SNAP to buy nutritious staples like beans, rice, eggs, and frozen vegetables.

## Myth: You Cannot Buy Seeds or Plants with SNAP

**Fact:** SNAP benefits can be used to purchase seeds and plants that produce food for the household to eat. This includes vegetable seeds, herb plants, and fruit-bearing plants. Growing your own food is one way to stretch your benefits further.

## Myth: Receiving SNAP Affects Immigration Status

**Fact:** According to USDA FNS, receiving SNAP benefits does not make someone a "public charge" for immigration purposes. U.S. citizens and many lawfully present non-citizens are eligible for SNAP. The 2022 public charge final rule specifically excludes SNAP from the benefits that are considered in public charge determinations. If you have immigration-related concerns, consult an immigration attorney or accredited representative for guidance specific to your situation.

## Myth: Working People Do Not Qualify for SNAP

**Fact:** Many SNAP households include at least one working adult. SNAP eligibility is based on household size, income, and certain expenses — not just employment status. According to USDA, the majority of SNAP participants who can work do work. Part-time workers, low-wage workers, and families with fluctuating income may all qualify.

## Myth: SNAP Benefits Can Be Used Anywhere

**Fact:** SNAP benefits can only be used at USDA-authorized retailers. Most grocery stores, supermarkets, and many farmers markets are authorized. However, you cannot use SNAP at restaurants (except in limited state-run Restaurant Meals Programs for elderly, disabled, or homeless individuals), gas stations that do not sell groceries, or online stores that are not part of the USDA online purchasing pilot.

*For official eligibility information and state-specific rules, contact your state SNAP office or visit [benefits.gov](https://benefits.gov).*
