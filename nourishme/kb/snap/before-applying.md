## Getting Ready to Apply

Applying for SNAP benefits is free and available in every state. According to USDA FNS, most applications can be submitted online through your state's benefits portal, in person at your local SNAP office, by mail, or by fax. Having your documents ready before you start can help the process go smoothly and avoid delays.

## Documents You Should Gather

While exact requirements vary by state, you will generally need:

- **Proof of identity** — driver's license, state ID, passport, or birth certificate for each household member
- **Social Security numbers** — for all household members applying (or proof of application if a number has not been issued yet)
- **Proof of income** — recent pay stubs (typically the last 30 days), employer statements, self-employment records, Social Security or disability award letters, child support received, or unemployment compensation
- **Proof of expenses** — rent or mortgage statements, utility bills, child care receipts, medical bills for elderly or disabled household members, and court-ordered child support payments
- **Proof of residency** — a lease, utility bill, or other document showing your current address
- **Bank statements** — some states consider liquid assets; others have eliminated the asset test for most households

If you are missing a document, apply anyway — you can often submit missing items after your initial application.

## What Happens After You Apply

Once your application is submitted, your state SNAP office will schedule an interview (often by phone). According to FNS, most applications are processed within 30 days. If your household has very low income and few resources, you may qualify for expedited (emergency) benefits within 7 days. After approval, you will receive an EBT card loaded with your monthly benefit amount.

*For official eligibility information and state-specific application details, contact your state SNAP office or visit [benefits.gov](https://benefits.gov).*
