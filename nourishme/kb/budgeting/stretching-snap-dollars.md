## Plan Before You Shop

One of the most effective ways to stretch SNAP benefits is to plan your meals before heading to the store. According to USDA guidance, families who plan meals and write a shopping list tend to spend less and waste less food. Start by checking what you already have in your pantry and fridge. Build your week's meals around those items and fill in gaps with affordable staples like rice, beans, eggs, and frozen vegetables — all of which are SNAP-eligible.

## Compare Prices Per Unit

Look at the unit price (price per ounce, per pound, or per count) on the shelf tag rather than the total package price. Larger packages are often cheaper per unit, but only buy more than you need if you can store it properly or freeze the extra. Store brands and generic labels frequently offer the same nutritional value as name brands at 20–40% lower cost. Canned and frozen fruits and vegetables are often cheaper than fresh and last longer — look for options packed in water or without added sugar.

## Take Advantage of Seasonal Produce and Programs

Fresh produce is usually cheapest when it is in season. In summer, look for tomatoes, zucchini, berries, and corn; in winter, opt for sweet potatoes, cabbage, carrots, and apples. Many farmers markets accept SNAP/EBT and some offer bonus programs — for example, USDA's GusNIP (Gus Schumacher Nutrition Incentive Program) and state-run programs like Double Up Food Bucks give you extra dollars to spend on fruits and vegetables when you use SNAP at participating markets.

*For official eligibility information and state-specific programs, contact your state SNAP office or visit [benefits.gov](https://benefits.gov).*
