## What Is Pantry-First Cooking?

Pantry-first cooking means building your meals around the ingredients you already have at home before heading to the store. According to USDA MyPlate guidance, planning meals around what is on hand reduces food waste and helps you spend less. Instead of finding a recipe and buying all the ingredients, you look at your pantry, fridge, and freezer and decide what you can make right now — then only buy the few items you are missing.

## Building a Versatile Pantry

A well-stocked pantry does not have to be expensive. Start with a core set of staples that you can combine in many ways: rice or pasta, dried or canned beans, canned tomatoes, oats, flour, cooking oil, and a few basic spices (salt, pepper, garlic powder, cumin, chili powder). These items are all SNAP-eligible and have a long shelf life, so you can buy them on sale and keep them for weeks or months. Add eggs, onions, and frozen vegetables for flexibility — frozen vegetables are often cheaper than fresh, last much longer, and are nutritionally comparable according to USDA research.

## Putting It Into Practice

When it is time to cook, check what you have first. Rice and canned beans can become a seasoned rice-and-bean bowl. Pasta plus canned tomatoes and garlic powder makes a simple marinara. Oats plus peanut butter and a banana is a filling breakfast. The idea is to think of your pantry staples as a starting point, not a limitation. If you are missing one ingredient, see if you can substitute something similar — broth instead of water, frozen spinach instead of fresh, lemon juice instead of vinegar. Over time, pantry-first cooking becomes a habit that saves money and reduces the number of items you need to buy each week.
