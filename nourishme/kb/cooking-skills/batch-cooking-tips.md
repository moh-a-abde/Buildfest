## What Is Batch Cooking?

Batch cooking means preparing larger quantities of food at one time so you have ready-made meals or components for several days. According to USDA MyPlate guidance, planning ahead and preparing meals in advance can save both time and money. Instead of cooking from scratch every night, you cook a few staple items on one day and mix them into different meals throughout the week.

## How to Get Started

Pick one or two hours on a day off to cook your staples. Good batch-cooking candidates include grains (rice, pasta, oats), proteins (chicken thighs, ground turkey, dried beans), and roasted vegetables. Cook a large pot of beans or a big tray of roasted veggies, divide into containers, and refrigerate or freeze portions. During the week, combine these components in different ways: beans over rice with salsa on Monday, bean soup on Wednesday, rice bowls with roasted vegetables on Friday.

## Budget Benefits

Batch cooking reduces food waste (a major cost driver) because you use ingredients before they spoil. Buying larger bags of rice, dried beans, or frozen vegetables is often cheaper per serving than buying small quantities. According to USDA estimates, a family of four can save 20–30% on weekly food costs by meal planning and batch cooking compared to buying pre-made or single-serving foods. All staple ingredients for batch cooking — rice, beans, vegetables, oil, spices — are SNAP-eligible.
