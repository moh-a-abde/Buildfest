## The Danger Zone

According to the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS), bacteria grow most rapidly between 40 °F and 140 °F — known as the "Danger Zone." Perishable food left in this temperature range for more than two hours (or one hour if the temperature is above 90 °F) should be discarded. Keep your refrigerator at 40 °F or below and your freezer at 0 °F or below.

## Refrigerator Storage Guidelines

Most leftovers are safe to eat within 3–4 days when stored in the refrigerator at 40 °F or below. Raw chicken and ground meats should be used within 1–2 days. Eggs in the shell keep for 3–5 weeks. Store raw meats on the lowest shelf to prevent juices from dripping onto other foods. Use clear containers or bags so you can see what is inside, and label them with the date.

## Freezer Storage for Budget Stretching

Freezing food is one of the best ways to prevent waste and stretch your grocery budget. According to USDA FSIS, food stored at 0 °F is safe indefinitely, though quality may decline over time. Bread, cooked rice, soups, and most fruits and vegetables freeze well. Divide large batches into meal-sized portions before freezing so you can thaw only what you need. All freezer-safe food containers and bags are available at SNAP-authorized retailers.
