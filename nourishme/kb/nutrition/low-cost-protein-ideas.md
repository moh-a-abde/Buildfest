## Why Protein Matters

Protein helps build and repair muscles, supports your immune system, and keeps you feeling satisfied after meals. According to USDA MyPlate guidance, most adults need about 5–7 ounce-equivalents of protein foods per day, depending on age, sex, and activity level. The good news is that many of the most affordable foods at the grocery store are excellent protein sources — you do not need expensive cuts of meat to meet your needs.

## Budget-Friendly Protein Sources

Dried beans and lentils are among the cheapest protein sources available. A one-pound bag of dried beans costs around $1–2 and provides roughly 10 servings, each with about 7–9 grams of protein. Eggs are another standout — a dozen eggs typically costs $2–4 and provides 12 servings of high-quality protein at about 6 grams each. Other affordable options include canned tuna or sardines, chicken thighs (often significantly cheaper per pound than chicken breasts), peanut butter, tofu, and canned chickpeas. According to the Dietary Guidelines for Americans, variety in protein sources is encouraged — mixing plant-based and animal-based proteins throughout the week is a practical and nutritious approach.

## Putting It Together on a SNAP Budget

All of these protein sources are SNAP-eligible. A simple strategy is to anchor two or three dinners per week around beans or lentils (like chili, bean soup, or rice and beans), use eggs for quick breakfasts or dinners, and reserve meat for one or two meals when it is on sale. Buying whole chickens or bone-in thighs and cooking them in batches stretches your dollars further than buying pre-cut or boneless portions. Canned beans are more convenient than dried but cost a bit more — rinsing canned beans also reduces their sodium content by about 40%, according to the USDA.
