## Understanding the Label

The Nutrition Facts label appears on most packaged foods sold in the United States. According to the FDA, it is designed to help you make informed food choices by showing key nutrients per serving. The label includes serving size, calories, and amounts of fat, sodium, carbohydrates, fiber, sugars, protein, and select vitamins and minerals.

## Key Sections to Check

**Serving size** is at the top — all numbers on the label are based on this amount. If you eat double the serving size, you are getting double the calories and nutrients listed. **% Daily Value (DV)** on the right side tells you how much a nutrient in one serving contributes to a daily diet based on 2,000 calories. As a general guide from the FDA: 5% DV or less is considered low, and 20% DV or more is considered high. Look for foods low in saturated fat, sodium, and added sugars, and higher in fiber, vitamin D, calcium, iron, and potassium.

## Tips for Budget Shoppers

Comparing labels can help you get more nutrition for your dollar. Store-brand products often have the same nutritional profile as name brands at a lower price. When buying canned goods with SNAP benefits, check for "no salt added" or "packed in water" versions — they often cost the same but have lower sodium.

*For personalized dietary advice, consult a healthcare provider or registered dietitian.*
