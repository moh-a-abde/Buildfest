## General Nutrition vs. Professional Guidance

General nutrition tips — like eating more vegetables, choosing whole grains, and balancing your plate — are safe and helpful for most people. However, certain health situations call for personalized advice from a qualified professional. According to the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, a registered dietitian nutritionist (RDN) is trained to create individualized eating plans that account for medical conditions, medications, and personal health goals. NourishMe can help you learn about nutrition basics and plan budget-friendly meals, but it is not a substitute for medical advice.

## When You Should Talk to a Professional

Consider reaching out to a doctor or registered dietitian if any of these apply to you or a family member: you have been diagnosed with a condition that affects what you eat, such as **diabetes**, **heart disease**, **kidney disease**, **celiac disease**, or **high blood pressure**; you have **food allergies** or suspect you might (NourishMe supports allergen exclusions in meal planning, but only a healthcare provider can diagnose an allergy and advise on safe management); you are **pregnant or breastfeeding** and want guidance on nutrition needs for you and your baby; you are experiencing **unintended weight changes** — significant gain or loss without a clear reason; you or someone in your household is struggling with **disordered eating** or has a difficult relationship with food; or a child in your household has **growth or feeding concerns**. In all of these cases, professional guidance ensures safety and avoids unintended harm.

## How to Find Help

Many community health centers offer nutrition counseling on a sliding fee scale, and some accept Medicaid. You can search for a registered dietitian near you at the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics website (eatright.org/find-a-nutrition-expert). The CDC also provides resources for managing chronic conditions through its chronic disease prevention programs. If cost is a concern, ask your doctor for a referral — nutrition counseling for certain conditions (like diabetes) is often covered by insurance, including Medicaid.

*For personalized dietary advice, medical conditions, or allergies, consult a healthcare provider or registered dietitian.*
