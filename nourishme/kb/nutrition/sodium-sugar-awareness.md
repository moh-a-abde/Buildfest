## Understanding Sodium

Sodium is a mineral found naturally in many foods, but most of the sodium Americans consume comes from processed and packaged foods. According to the Dietary Guidelines for Americans, the recommended daily limit is less than 2,300 milligrams for adults — about one teaspoon of table salt. The FDA notes that most Americans consume closer to 3,400 mg per day. Eating too much sodium over time is linked to higher blood pressure, which can increase the risk of heart disease and stroke. Being aware of sodium does not mean you have to give up flavor — it just means paying attention to where it shows up.

## Understanding Added Sugars

Added sugars are sugars and syrups added during food processing or preparation. They are different from the natural sugars found in whole fruits and plain milk. According to the FDA, the updated Nutrition Facts label now lists "Added Sugars" separately, making it easier to see how much sugar has been added to a product. The Dietary Guidelines recommend that added sugars make up less than 10% of daily calories. Common sources include sodas, fruit drinks, candy, baked goods, and sweetened cereals. Choosing water, plain milk, or unsweetened beverages more often is one of the simplest changes you can make.

## Practical Tips for Budget Shoppers

You do not need to overhaul your diet — small shifts can make a difference. When buying canned vegetables or beans, look for "no salt added" or "low sodium" versions, which often cost the same as regular. Rinsing canned beans and vegetables under water can reduce sodium by up to 40%, according to the USDA. For added sugars, compare labels on cereals, yogurts, and sauces — store brands with less added sugar are often the same price or cheaper. Cooking at home with basic spices and herbs (garlic, onion powder, cumin, black pepper) adds flavor without extra sodium. All of these items are SNAP-eligible.

*For personalized dietary advice, consult a healthcare provider or registered dietitian.*
