## What Is MyPlate?

MyPlate is the USDA's current guide to building balanced meals. It replaced the older Food Pyramid in 2011 and uses a simple plate graphic to show how much of each food group to aim for at each meal. According to USDA guidance at MyPlate.gov, a balanced plate is roughly half fruits and vegetables, about a quarter grains, and about a quarter protein, with a small side of dairy.

## The Five Food Groups

The five MyPlate food groups are **Fruits**, **Vegetables**, **Grains**, **Protein**, and **Dairy**. Fruits and vegetables should take up the largest portion of your plate — they provide vitamins, minerals, and fiber. At least half of your grains should be whole grains (like brown rice, whole-wheat bread, or oatmeal). Protein sources include meat, poultry, seafood, beans, peas, eggs, nuts, and seeds. Dairy includes milk, yogurt, and cheese, or calcium-fortified alternatives.

## Practical Tips

You do not need to follow MyPlate perfectly at every meal. The goal is a general pattern over time. Start by adding one extra serving of vegetables or fruit to a meal you already make. Frozen and canned fruits and vegetables count — look for options without added sugar or salt. All of these items are SNAP-eligible, making MyPlate a practical framework for budget-friendly healthy eating.

*For personalized dietary advice, consult a healthcare provider or registered dietitian.*
