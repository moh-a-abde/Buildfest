## What Is Fiber?

Fiber is a type of carbohydrate found in plant foods that your body cannot fully digest. Unlike sugars and starches, fiber passes through the digestive system mostly intact. According to the Dietary Guidelines for Americans, most people do not eat enough fiber — the recommended daily amount is about 25 grams for women and 38 grams for men, but the average American gets only about 15 grams per day.

## Why Fiber Matters

Eating enough fiber supports digestive health, helps you feel full longer after meals, and may help manage blood sugar levels. High-fiber diets are also associated with lower risk of heart disease and certain other chronic conditions, according to the USDA Dietary Guidelines. Fiber-rich foods also tend to be nutrient-dense, providing vitamins, minerals, and other beneficial compounds.

## Budget-Friendly High-Fiber Foods

Many of the most affordable foods at the grocery store are naturally high in fiber. Dried beans and lentils are among the cheapest per-serving sources of fiber — a one-pound bag of dried pinto beans costs around $1–2 and provides over 10 servings. Oats, brown rice, whole-wheat bread, frozen vegetables, and fresh fruits like bananas and apples are also good sources. All of these are SNAP-eligible purchases.
