## The Idea Behind Swaps

Healthy swaps are not about labeling foods as "good" or "bad." According to USDA MyPlate guidance, the goal is to find small, practical changes that fit your life and budget — not to follow rigid rules. A swap is simply choosing a similar option that adds a bit more nutrition to a meal you already enjoy. You do not have to make every swap every time; even one or two changes a week can add up over time.

## Swaps to Try

Here are some ideas based on the Dietary Guidelines for Americans. **Grains**: Try whole-wheat bread or brown rice in place of white versions when you can — whole grains have more fiber and nutrients. **Beverages**: Water is always the most affordable drink; if you currently drink soda or juice regularly, try replacing one serving a day with water or unsweetened iced tea. **Dairy**: Plain yogurt has less added sugar than flavored varieties — you can add your own fruit or a small drizzle of honey to control sweetness. **Seasoning**: Herbs and spices (garlic powder, cumin, oregano, lemon juice) can add flavor to meals in place of extra salt. **Snacks**: Fresh or frozen fruit, air-popped popcorn, or a handful of nuts can be satisfying alternatives to chips or cookies when you are looking for a change.

## Budget-Friendly Tips

Many of these swaps cost the same or less than the original. Store-brand whole-wheat bread is often priced close to white bread. Frozen fruit is cheaper than fresh and works well in oatmeal or smoothies. Buying plain yogurt in a large tub instead of individual flavored cups saves money and lets you control what goes in. Dried herbs and spices from the international aisle or bulk bins are significantly cheaper than name-brand spice jars. All of these items are SNAP-eligible purchases.

*For personalized dietary advice, consult a healthcare provider or registered dietitian.*
